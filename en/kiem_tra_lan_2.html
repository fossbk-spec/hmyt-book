3. Traceability rules ​

  1. Confirmation comment (one per team):

    text
    Team: <team_code> · Topic: <topic_slug>
Fork: <fork URL>
Branch: gd5-proposed-model
Commit: <full 40-character SHA>

    Only comments posted between 7:00 and 9:00 on Tuesday 10/11/2026 count, by GitHub's timestamp. To fix the SHA within that window, edit your comment; do not post a new one.

  2. The instructor grades the file at the exact SHA in your comment. Later commits do not count for this test. Do not force-push away the submitted commit.

  3. Every claim needs evidence: the DOI or link of the paper, the path to the notebook or script, the W&B/TensorBoard link, or the path to the result file. A claim without evidence does not count.

  4. No full names. Use student IDs, aliases, or roles only. No real patient data (see the Management Framework & Rubric).

  5. Numbers in the results table must match real output in the repo: notebooks, logs, CSVs. A number that cannot be traced to its source counts as missing.

5. What the instructor checks in the oral ​

PartThe instructor checks
A. Literature and gapsPapers are real and DOIs are correct. Quoted numbers match the original paper. Each gap is backed by the matrix, not a generic statement. Members can explain the papers they were responsible for reading
B. Results and timelineNumbers trace back to output in the repo. A baseline exists for comparison. The comparison with the literature states what is not equivalent. The timeline has owners and evidence
C. Self-assessmentThe reporting guideline matches the research question. PROBAST+AI answers have concrete justification. The team recognises the risks of its own project, especially leakage, incorporation bias, sample size, and calibration

A team that identifies and clearly states a weakness in its own project is rated higher than a team that marks every item "Met" without evidence.
