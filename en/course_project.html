COURSE PROJECT MANAGEMENT FRAMEWORK & GRADING RUBRIC ​

📥 English version notice

This page summarizes the project management framework, milestones, core policies, and the full bilingual topic bank (15 topics). Part III's detailed 3-tier grading tables (Excellent/Good/Needs improvement — 5 large tables, dozens of rows) are condensed here to weight + one-line criteria for readability; the complete row-by-row rubric text is in the Vietnamese version.

Course: Machine Learning in Healthcare (ET4248) — 3 credits (Project weight: 60%) ​

Instructors: Dr. Hoang Quang Huy, Dr. Tran Anh Vu Applies to: Biomedical Engineering, Information Technology, and Biomedical Electronics students taking the course project.

Part I — Project Management Framework ​

The course follows Project-Based Learning (PBL) with continuous assessment — the project is not graded only at the end of the semester, but tracked through Git/GitHub across staged milestones.

Core principles for biomedical project management ​

  1. Single Source of Truth (SSoT) on Git: all documents (reports, literature matrix, weekly meeting minutes) and code (data pipeline, model, evaluation scripts) must live in the team's GitHub repository.
  2. Medical safety & Zero Data Leakage rule:
    • Patient-level splitting (GroupKFold or equivalent on patient_id) — never split randomly at the record level.
    • Any scaler fit_transform runs only on Train; Test only gets transform.
  3. Zero PII/PHI compliance: strictly follows HIPAA-equivalent principles and Vietnam's Decree 13/2023/NĐ-CP. Never publish real patient names, addresses, national ID numbers, or insurance codes to GitHub — use de-identified or licensed open data only.
  4. Zero-Freerider policy: each student's grade equals the team grade multiplied by a Peer Contribution Coefficient (), computed from Git commit/PR history and a confidential peer-review vote.

Part IV — Strict Penalty Policies ​

  1. Data leakage violation: if Train/Test leakage is found (e.g. the same patient in both sets, or scaling before the split): −50% of the milestone's score. The team must fix and resubmit the PR within 3 days.
  2. PII/PHI breach: publishing real, non-de-identified patient data to the public GitHub repo: project acceptance suspended and −30 points directly from the final course grade.
  3. "Freerider" behavior: any member with no commits/PRs on Git, or rated by the rest of the team: 0 points on that milestone's technical portion.
  4. Late submission: −10% per 24 hours late; submissions more than 72 hours late are not accepted (0 for that milestone).
  5. Academic fraud & hallucinated citations (Zero-Hallucination Policy): copying a prior cohort's/another team's project without disclosure; fabricating experimental results; AI-hallucinated citations: project result voided (0 points) and referred to the university Disciplinary Committee.

Part V — Team Roles & Peer Assessment ​

Uses the international CRediT (Contributor Roles Taxonomy) standard for a 3-4 student team: Project Lead & ML Architect, Data & Preprocessing Specialist, Evaluation & XAI Specialist, Documentation & MLOps Engineer. Part VI — Project Topic Bank (4 biomedical modalities) ​

Choose one of the standardized topics below, or propose an equivalent. 🇬🇧 = full English detail page available; otherwise the link opens the Vietnamese detail page (still usable — tables, code, DOIs are language-neutral).

1. Biomedical Signals & Time-series ​

Applies: Ch.3 (Regression), Ch.4 (Evaluation & Data Leakage), Ch.7 (Neural Networks & LSTM).

  • Topic 1.1: Predicting acute decompensated heart failure risk from vital-sign time series and weight data.
    • Dataset: MIMIC-III/IV (cardiovascular patient time series extracted) or simulated data.
    • Reference model: LSTM, XGBoost for time series; Baseline: Random Forest on hand-crafted features.
    • Biomedical challenge: Handling irregular sampling frequency in clinical time series; early warning 24-48h ahead.
    • 📄 Literature review & workflow → 🇬🇧
  • Topic 1.2: Multi-label arrhythmia classification from 12-lead ECG signals.
    • Dataset: PTB-XL Dataset (PhysioNet) - 21,837 12-lead ECG records.
    • Reference model: 1D-CNN, ResNet-1D; Baseline: Logistic Regression.
    • Biomedical challenge: Severe class imbalance across disease categories; splitting must be independent by `patient_id`.
    • 📄 Literature review & workflow → 🇬🇧

2. Medical Imaging (Fundus / Dermatology) ​

Applies: Ch.4 (ROC-AUC), Ch.7 (Deep Learning), Ch.8 (CNNs).

  • Topic 2.1: Segmenting and measuring hives (wheals) from skin photographs to support Urticaria management.
    • Dataset: Self-collected dermatology images with a size reference object (ruler), or open-source dermoscopy datasets.
    • Reference model: U-Net, Mask R-CNN; Baseline: color-thresholding segmentation.
    • Biomedical challenge: Hairy skin, lighting noise; converting pixels to real-world size (cm) to compute wheal surface-area scores.
    • 📄 Literature review & workflow → 🇬🇧
  • Topic 2.2: Grading Diabetic Retinopathy (DR) severity from fundus photographs.
    • Dataset: APTOS 2019 Blindness Detection (Kaggle).
    • Reference model: DenseNet-121, EfficientNet; Baseline: vanilla ResNet-18.
    • Biomedical challenge: XAI required: **Grad-CAM** heatmaps must highlight microaneurysms and retinal hemorrhages.
    • 📄 Literature review & workflow → 🇬🇧
  • Topic 2.3: Automatic Cup-to-Disc Ratio (CDR) measurement from fundus images for glaucoma screening.
    • Dataset: REFUGE Challenge or RIM-ONE.
    • Biomedical challenge: Accurately segmenting the optic disc and cup boundaries to compute a precise geometric ratio.
    • 📄 Literature review & workflow → 🇬🇧
  • Topic 2.4: Classifying Age-related Macular Degeneration (AMD) from fundus photographs.
    • Dataset: AREDS (Age-Related Eye Disease Study), iChallenge-AMD.
    • Reference model: Multi-class CNN (normal/intermediate/geographic atrophy/wet AMD); Baseline: vanilla ResNet-18.
    • Biomedical challenge: XAI required: Grad-CAM/SHAP must highlight drusen, hemorrhages, and geographic atrophy regions.
    • 📄 Literature review & workflow → 🇬🇧
  • Topic 2.5: Detecting Epiretinal Membrane (ERM) from fundus photographs or OCT.
    • Dataset: Self-collected fundus images with ERM (public data is scarce), or public OCT data as a methodological benchmark.
    • Biomedical challenge: Literature on color fundus images (as opposed to OCT) is very scarce — requires investing more effort in literature search/data collection from the start.
    • 📄 Literature review & workflow → 🇬🇧
  • Topic 2.6: (advanced — unified): Multi-label classification of several retinal diseases (DR, AMD, Glaucoma...) simultaneously with a SINGLE Edge AI model running fully offline on a mobile device — the closest simulation of the real engineering problem behind an AI-integrated portable fundus camera.
    • Dataset: ODIR-5K (Ocular Disease Intelligent Recognition) combined with self-collected data.
    • Reference model: MobileNet-Lite, Quantized EfficientNet-Lite; Baseline: vanilla ResNet-18 or DenseNet.
    • Biomedical challenge: Optimize model size and offline inference speed on-phone via INT8 quantization; handle the multi-label setting where one image may show multiple diseases at once. **Grad-CAM** heatmaps required per lesion type.
    • 📄 Literature review & workflow → 🇬🇧
  • Topic 2.7: Automated fundus Image Quality Assessment (IQA) — a quality gate before diagnosis.
    • Dataset: EyeQ (based on EyePACS/Kaggle, 28,792 images, 3 quality levels: Good/Usable/Reject).
    • Reference model: CNN / Autoencoder+Classifier (see FGR-Net), Grad-CAM to explain rejection reasons.
    • Biomedical challenge: Distinguishing Usable vs Reject is harder than Good vs Reject; must explain WHY an image is rejected, not just output a label.
    • 📄 Literature review & workflow → 🇬🇧

3. Clinical Tabular Data & EHR ​

Applies: Ch.2 (Preprocessing & Missing Data), Ch.3 (Odds Ratio), Ch.4 (Regularization), Ch.5 (SVM/Feature Selection).

  • Topic 3.1: Personalizing the "Four Pillars" Heart Failure regimen for frail elderly patients.
    • Dataset: Extracted from public EHR repositories (e.g. MIMIC) with EF, eGFR, and serum potassium available.
    • Reference model: Random Forest, XGBoost; Baseline: multi-class Logistic Regression.
    • Biomedical challenge: Explainability-focused: use **SHAP** to explain why ARNI is withheld due to hyperkalemia risk or reduced eGFR.
    • 📄 Literature review & workflow → 🇬🇧
  • Topic 3.2: Handling missing cognitive-score data (MMSE/MoCA) in Dementia screening.
    • Dataset: ADNI (Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative) or OASIS.
    • Reference model: MICE (Multiple Imputation), KNN Imputer.
    • Biomedical challenge: Imputing data without causing "Artificial Normalcy" that would bias the MCI vs Dementia diagnostic boundary.
    • 📄 Literature review & workflow → 🇬🇧
  • Topic 3.3: Building a fall-risk and Anticholinergic Burden warning system (DDI-AI).
    • Dataset: Simulated polypharmacy data based on elderly patient records.
    • Biomedical challenge: Combining a rule engine with machine learning to hard-stop dangerous psychotropic drug interactions.
    • 📄 Literature review & workflow → 🇬🇧
  • Topic 3.4: Mortality/complication risk stratification for Heart Failure patients from tabular data (Tabular DxAI) — ACCELERATED version.
    • Dataset: Heart Failure Clinical Records dataset (UCI/Kaggle) - 299 patients, no access request needed.
    • Reference model: Random Forest, Extra Trees + SMOTE; Baseline: Logistic Regression.
    • Biomedical challenge: Apply **SHAP** to explain key drivers (Serum Creatinine, Ejection Fraction); handle label imbalance with SMOTE on Train only. The easiest/fastest topic in the bank — completed in 5 sessions (~10-15h) instead of 15 weeks.
    • 📄 Literature review & workflow → 🇬🇧

4. Clinical Text Mining & Medical Reports (NLP) ​

Applies: Ch.6 (Embeddings), Ch.7 (Neural Networks).

  • Topic 4.1: Automatically extracting Urticaria trigger context from free-text clinical history.
    • Dataset: Unstructured clinical notes (MIMIC-IV notes) mentioning allergy/urticaria.
    • Reference model: ClinicalBERT, BioBERT; Baseline: TF-IDF + SVM.
    • Biomedical challenge: Named Entity Recognition (NER) for complex trigger factors (food, weather, stress) plus clinical negation detection.
    • 📄 Literature review & workflow → 🇬🇧
  • Topic 4.2: Automatic ICD-10 code classification from hospital discharge summaries.
    • Dataset: MIMIC-IV Clinical Database (Demo subset).
    • Biomedical challenge: Extremely large label space (ICD-10 codes) plus non-standardized medical abbreviations.
    • 📄 Literature review & workflow → 🇬🇧
