2.2. TECHNICAL MOTIVATION (TECHNICAL MOTIVATION) ​

2.2.1. The "Garbage In, Garbage Out" (GIGO) Principle in Medical AI ​

In computer science and artificial intelligence, the classic "Garbage In, Garbage Out" (GIGO) principle asserts that even the most sophisticated deep-learning or machine-learning architectures will produce biased, meaningless, or dangerous predictions if the input data is contaminated by noise, measurement error, or bias.

In a healthcare context, GIGO does not merely lower a model's technical score — it directly threatens patient safety:

  • An ECG sensor with a loose electrode can generate a noisy waveform that fools an AI model into misdiagnosing paroxysmal ventricular fibrillation, leading to an erroneous decision to defibrillate.
  • A blood-glucose reading with a mistyped decimal point can lead to an insulin-dosing order that causes a fatal hypoglycemic coma.

2.5. RUBIN'S MISSING-DATA THEORY & IMPUTATION STRATEGIES (MISSING DATA THEORY) ​

In electronic health records, missing data is the norm, not the exception. Physicians never order every single one of the hundreds of possible tests for every patient; which test is ordered depends closely on the patient's condition and clinical judgment.

2.5.1. Rubin's Theoretical Framework (1976): Three Missingness Mechanisms ​

  1. Missing Completely at Random (MCAR):

    • Nature: The probability that a value is missing is entirely independent of both the observed values and the missing value itself.
    • Clinical example: A blood-sample tube is accidentally dropped and broken in the lab; a random 5-minute network disconnection between a monitor and the server.
    • Handling: Deleting incomplete rows (Complete Case Analysis) or Mean Imputation introduces no systematic bias (unbiased), although it does reduce sample size.

  2. Missing at Random (MAR):

    • Nature: The probability that a value is missing depends on the patient's observed information (), but is independent of the (unmeasured) value itself ().
    • Clinical example: Physicians rarely order a cardiac Troponin test for young patients ( years old) with no chest-pain symptoms; the probability of a missing Troponin depends on the "Age" and "Symptom" variables (already recorded in the chart).
    • Another clinical example (Heart Failure): the NT-proBNP test (a heart-failure biomarker) is usually only ordered when the patient has clear dyspnea or peripheral edema — the missingness probability depends on already-observed functional-symptom variables, not on the true NT-proBNP value (unknown because unmeasured).
    • Another clinical example (Urticaria): advanced immunological tests such as ASST (Autologous Serum Skin Test), BAT (Basophil Activation Test), and quantitative Anti-TPO are only ordered once the physician already suspects an autoimmune urticaria subtype (CIndU/CSU) from the clinical history — a textbook case of MAR conditional on the "suspected clinical subgroup" variable.
    • Handling: Deleting rows introduces serious bias. Multivariate imputation algorithms such as KNN Imputer or MICE must be used.

  3. Missing Not at Random (MNAR):

    • Nature: The probability that data is missing depends directly on the value of the missing variable itself.
    • Clinical example: A patient with severe depression refuses to answer a mood-assessment questionnaire; a patient with such profoundly low blood pressure that they are comatose at the scene cannot have their standing blood pressure measured.
    • Handling: This is the most dangerous form of missingness. No automatic imputation algorithm is entirely safe. Solution: add a binary Missingness Indicator () so the model can learn the actual clinical meaning of "this test was not performed."

2.5.3. Clinical Pitfall: the "Artificial Normalcy" Phenomenon ​

A very common mistake made by data engineers processing EHR tables is to automatically fill missing lab results with the mean value of a healthy population (Normal Reference Mean) — for example, imputing blood Lactate or Troponin for every case where the test was not ordered.

A fatal clinical consequence:

  • In reality, an Emergency Department patient who was not ordered a Troponin test may simply have been judged by the physician to have low cardiovascular risk. However, if a patient is actually experiencing critical acute pancreatitis but has not yet had the test done, assigning a normal value will cause the AI model to wrongly conclude the patient is entirely healthy — leading to Triage Underestimation.
  • Golden rule: Always pair imputation with the creation of an additional binary flag variable, is_missing_feature, so the algorithm can tell whether the physician actually ordered that test in the first place.

2.5.4. Real-World Application: the Data Completeness Tiering Algorithm ​

In clinical deployment, the MCAR/MAR/MNAR theoretical framework is often operationalized into a Data Completeness Tiering algorithm to decide which records are reliable enough for model training/validation. An example applied to a dementia (Alzheimer's) screening problem:

Every published model must transparently report the percentage of records by Tier in its Model Card — a mandatory accountability practice when working with imperfect medical data, instead of silently discarding or imputing records without documentation.

2.6. THE 4-STEP BIOMEDICAL DATA PREPROCESSING PIPELINE (DATA PIPELINE) ​

A standard biomedical data preprocessing pipeline consists of 4 sequential, closed stages, illustrated in the architecture diagram below:

Step 1: LOAD (Loading & Integrating Multi-source Data) ​

  • Extract data from specialized formats (DICOM for images, EDF for waveform signals, FHIR JSON for health records).
  • Merge data sources using the patient's unique identifier (Master Patient Index - MPI).

  • Check the missingness rate per column and per row. Drop variables with a missing rate unless the variable is life-critical.
  • Check for imbalance in the target label ().

Step 3: CLEAN (Cleaning & Imputing Data) ​

  • Apply Physiological Plausibility Rules: remove values caused by entry errors (e.g., a heart rate or ).
  • Classify the missingness mechanism per Rubin's framework, and apply the corresponding imputation strategy (KNN Imputer / MICE).
  • Generate derived features (Feature Engineering) and missingness-indicator variables.

Step 4: RESCALE (Feature Scaling & Variable Encoding) ​

  • Scale continuous variables using RobustScaler (for skewed/outlier-prone variables) or StandardScaler.
  • Encode discrete categorical variables: OneHotEncoder for nominal variables (Blood type: A, B, AB, O) or OrdinalEncoder for ordered variables (NYHA heart-failure class: I, II, III, IV).
  • Discipline constraint: Fit every transformer exactly once, on the Training set, then apply transform() to the Test set.

2.7.2. The HL7 v2 and HL7 FHIR Messaging Standards ​

  • HL7 v2 (Health Level Seven version 2): A legacy standard using text messages delimited by special characters (| and ^).
    • The MSH segment (Message Header): version information, sending/receiving system.
    • The PID segment (Patient Identification): patient identifier, date of birth, gender.
    • The OBR segment (Observation Request): the test order request.
    • The OBX segment (Observation/Result): the specific measurement result, with units.
  • HL7 FHIR (Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources - Release 4/5) [@hl7_fhir]: The next-generation standard, based on a RESTful Web Services architecture and the JSON / XML format. Every medical entity is an independent "Resource" with its own URL address:
    • Patient: contains demographic data.
    • Observation: stores vital signs, biochemistry test results, and clinical scores.
    • Condition: records pathological diagnoses.
    • DiagnosticReport: imaging and pathology diagnostic conclusions.

2.7.3. The LOINC & SNOMED-CT International Nomenclature Coding Systems ​

  • LOINC (Logical Observation Identifiers Names and Codes) [@loinc_standard]: An international nomenclature standard that provides a unified identifier for medical tests and clinical indices.
    • Example: the code 2345-7 is the LOINC code identifying "Glucose in Serum/Plasma". Whether a hospital labels the test in Vietnamese, English, or French, it is mapped to the single code 2345-7, allowing an AI system to automatically aggregate multi-center data.
  • SNOMED-CT (Systematized Nomenclature of Medicine - Clinical Terms): The world's most comprehensive multilingual clinical vocabulary, encoding pathology concepts, clinical symptoms, and surgical procedures.

import sys

    sys.stdout.reconfigure(encoding='utf-8')

import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
from sklearn.model_selection import train_test_split
from sklearn.compose import ColumnTransformer
from sklearn.pipeline import Pipeline
from sklearn.impute import KNNImputer, SimpleImputer
from sklearn.preprocessing import StandardScaler, RobustScaler, OneHotEncoder
from sklearn.linear_model import LogisticRegression
from sklearn.metrics import classification_report, roc_auc_score

# ---------------------------------------------------------
# 1. SIMULATED CLINICAL DATASET (500 EMERGENCY-DEPARTMENT PATIENTS)
# ---------------------------------------------------------
np.random.seed(42)
n_samples = 500

# 1.1 Demographic & vital-sign features
age = np.random.normal(loc=62, scale=12, size=n_samples).clip(18, 95)
gender = np.random.choice(['Male', 'Female'], size=n_samples, p=[0.55, 0.45])
sbp = np.random.normal(loc=135, scale=20, size=n_samples)  # systolic blood pressure

# 1.2 Skewed biochemistry tests with outliers
glucose = np.random.lognormal(mean=4.8, sigma=0.4, size=n_samples)  # Glucose (mg/dL)
alt = np.random.exponential(scale=35, size=n_samples) + 15  # liver enzyme ALT (U/L)
troponin = np.random.exponential(scale=0.08, size=n_samples)  # cardiac enzyme (ng/mL)

# 1.3 Generate the Acute Coronary Syndrome target label
prob = 1 / (1 + np.exp(-(0.04*age + 0.02*sbp + 12*troponin + 0.01*glucose - 7.5)))
y = np.random.binomial(1, prob, size=n_samples)

# 1.4 Inject missingness following realistic mechanisms
# - Glucose: MCAR (8% randomly missing due to a data-transmission error)
glucose_mask = np.random.rand(n_samples) < 0.08
glucose[glucose_mask] = np.nan

# - Troponin: MAR (physicians order Troponin depending on blood pressure and age)
mar_prob = 1 / (1 + np.exp(-(0.05*age + 0.02*sbp - 6)))
troponin_mask = np.random.rand(n_samples) > mar_prob  # younger patients tested less often
troponin[troponin_mask] = np.nan

# 1.5 Inject physiologically implausible outliers (entry errors)
sbp[10] = 999.0  # blood-pressure typo
alt[25] = 4500.0  # fulminant hepatitis patient (a true pathological outlier)

df = pd.DataFrame({
    'patient_id': [f"PT_{i:04d}" for i in range(n_samples)],
    'age': age,
    'gender': gender,
    'sbp': sbp,
    'glucose': glucose,
    'alt': alt,
    'troponin': troponin,
    'target': y
})

print("=" * 70)
print("1. INITIAL ELECTRONIC HEALTH RECORD OVERVIEW (EHR DATASET):")
print(df.head())
print("\nMissing-data rate statistics (%):")
print(df.isnull().mean() * 100)
print("=" * 70)

# ---------------------------------------------------------
# 2. CLEANING PHYSIOLOGICALLY IMPLAUSIBLE OUTLIERS (RULE-BASED CLEANING)
# ---------------------------------------------------------
# Systolic blood pressure sbp > 300 mmHg is biologically implausible -> set to NaN
df.loc[df['sbp'] > 300, 'sbp'] = np.nan

# ---------------------------------------------------------
# 3. PATIENT-LEVEL SPLIT (TRAIN / TEST SPLIT)
# ---------------------------------------------------------
X = df[['age', 'gender', 'sbp', 'glucose', 'alt', 'troponin']]
y = df['target'].values

X_train, X_test, y_train, y_test = train_test_split(
    X, y, test_size=0.25, stratify=y, random_state=42
)

# ---------------------------------------------------------
# 4. BUILDING THE LEAKAGE-FREE PIPELINE WITH SCIKIT-LEARN
# ---------------------------------------------------------
# Define the column groups
skewed_features = ['alt', 'glucose', 'troponin']  # need RobustScaler & KNN Imputer
normal_features = ['age', 'sbp']                  # need StandardScaler & SimpleImputer
categorical_features = ['gender']                 # need OneHotEncoder

# Pipeline for skewed variables (Robust)
skewed_pipeline = Pipeline(steps=[
    ('imputer', KNNImputer(n_neighbors=5)),
    ('scaler', RobustScaler())
])

# Pipeline for normally distributed variables (Gaussian)
normal_pipeline = Pipeline(steps=[
    ('imputer', SimpleImputer(strategy='median')),
    ('scaler', StandardScaler())
])

# Pipeline for categorical variables
categorical_pipeline = Pipeline(steps=[
    ('imputer', SimpleImputer(strategy='most_frequent')),
    ('encoder', OneHotEncoder(drop='first', handle_unknown='ignore'))
])

# Merge via ColumnTransformer
preprocessor = ColumnTransformer(transformers=[
    ('skewed', skewed_pipeline, skewed_features),
    ('normal', normal_pipeline, normal_features),
    ('cat', categorical_pipeline, categorical_features)
])

# The full Pipeline, wrapping both preprocessing and the model
full_pipeline = Pipeline(steps=[
    ('preprocessor', preprocessor),
    ('classifier', LogisticRegression(class_weight='balanced', random_state=42))
])

# ---------------------------------------------------------
# 5. TRAINING AND EVALUATION (A SINGLE FIT ON TRAIN)
# ---------------------------------------------------------
full_pipeline.fit(X_train, y_train)

y_pred = full_pipeline.predict(X_test)
y_prob = full_pipeline.predict_proba(X_test)[:, 1]

print("\n2. PIPELINE EVALUATION RESULTS ON THE INDEPENDENT TEST SET (N=125):")
print(f"  - ROC-AUC Score: {roc_auc_score(y_test, y_prob):.4f}")
print("\nDETAILED CLASSIFICATION REPORT:")
print(classification_report(y_test, y_pred, target_names=['No Event', 'Acute Coronary Syndrome']))
print("=" * 70)
print("[+] PIPELINE EXECUTED SUCCESSFULLY: NO DATA LEAKAGE, MULTIMODAL PROCESSING!")

  1. Scaler Leakage: Calling StandardScaler.fit() or MinMaxScaler.fit() on the entire dataset before calling train_test_split() or KFold. In this case, the Test set's have already been revealed to the model.
  2. Imputer Leakage: Using the full data matrix to run KNNImputer or MICE. Data points from the Test set have directly participated in "voting" on the imputed values used for the Train set.

Golden Rules to Remember in Biomedical Data Preprocessing: ​

  1. Medical data always carries structure and clinical meaning: never mechanically delete or impute data without understanding its generating mechanism (MCAR/MAR/MNAR).
  2. Never fit a Scaler or Imputer on the entire dataset: every preprocessing parameter estimate may only be performed on the Training set, to prevent Data Leakage.
  3. Prefer robust statistics (Median, IQR, RobustScaler): clinical biochemistry data always has skewed distributions and genuine pathological outliers; using the Mean and 3-Sigma is often seriously misleading.
  4. Security and ethics are a prerequisite: always de-identify data per the HIPAA Safe Harbor standard, and comply with the 2025 Personal Data Protection Law & Decree 356/2025/NĐ-CP (replacing Decree 13/2023/NĐ-CP from 01/01/2026) before conducting research.
